# Polish naturalness diagnostics

Use these patterns as contextual prompts, not as authorship tests or deletion rules. Correct the text because a construction harms this reader and purpose, not because it appears on an “AI words” list.

## Discourse and rhythm

Look for empty openings such as *W dzisiejszych czasach…*, conclusions that merely restate the body, repeated paragraph-initial *Ponadto*, *Co więcej*, or *Warto zauważyć, że*, perfectly parallel sections, and sentences or paragraphs with mechanically uniform structure.

Lead with the substantive claim when framing adds no meaning. Delete an empty reminder frame instead of merely shortening it: *Należy pamiętać, że termin mija jutro* usually becomes *Termin mija jutro*. Remove semantic restatement; retain a second sentence when it adds a consequence, limitation, example, or useful emphasis. Vary rhythm only where monotony harms reading—never enforce a fixed sentence length or random fragmentation.

## Lexis, collocations, and voice

Question unsupported evaluatives such as *kluczowy*, *kompleksowy*, *innowacyjny*, *intuicyjny*, or *niezwykle skuteczny*. Do not preserve them merely because the user asked to keep facts: an unattributed promotional judgment is not automatically a factual invariant. Replace it with source-supported facts when those facts exist; otherwise delete or qualify it without inventing evidence. Retain approved claims, quotations, deliberate brand voice, and requested wording.

Prefer stable terminology over synonym rotation. Check suspicious verb–noun combinations, case government, idioms, and English-shaped phrases against meaning and register. For example, prefer *podjąć decyzję* to the calque *zrobić decyzję* and use *ma znaczenie* rather than mechanically translating *makes a difference*.

Use particles such as *właśnie*, *przecież*, *chyba*, *akurat*, or colloquial *no* only when the established speaker, relationship, and channel warrant them. Never sprinkle particles, slang, typos, or anecdotes into a text to simulate a human writer.

## Syntax and morphology

- Replace stacked nominalizations with finite verbs when no protected procedural term is lost.
- Name the actor when known, relevant, and appropriate; retain passive or impersonal forms in academic, technical, legal, and result-focused contexts when natural.
- Keep related words close enough to avoid unclear attachment.
- Treat subject–verb–object as a neutral baseline, not a mandatory order. Preserve marked order when it carries contrast, focus, cadence, or literary effect.
- Check agreement, government, case, reflexive possession, aspect, tense, clitic placement, and antecedents in context.
- Do not move every adjective before its noun. Characterizing adjectives often precede nouns; classifying or contrastive adjectives may naturally follow them.

## Orthography, punctuation, and typography

Apply the current *Zasady pisowni i interpunkcji polskiej*, effective from 1 January 2026, as the normative authority. Treat plain-language advice as genre guidance rather than universal grammar.

- Use Polish quotation marks `„…”` in ordinary Polish typography unless a supplied style guide requires another convention. Determine comma, period, question-mark, and exclamation-mark placement from whether the punctuation belongs to the quotation or the surrounding sentence.
- Distinguish the hyphen (`-`) inside compounds from a sentence or dialogue dash. Do not claim that one dash glyph is inherently non-Polish: choose a consistent dash form and spacing according to its function and the document's house style.
- Do not insert a comma after a simple initial connective or adverbial merely because speech permits a pause: *Jednak wynik…*, *W takim przypadku system…*. Preserve commas around genuine parenthetical insertions and where syntax requires them.
- Use sentence-style capitalization in headings unless the house style specifies otherwise. Preserve intentional names, acronyms, code, and branded capitalization.
- Remove duplicated spaces and punctuation-spacing errors outside protected code, URLs, citations, and quotations.
