# Editing priorities

Resolve conflicts in this order:

1. Follow the request: purpose, audience, genre, channel, register, scope, and format.
2. Preserve meaning and evidence.
3. Serve the reader with a clear information order and suitable level of explanation.
4. Improve naturalness, grammar, rhythm, punctuation, and typography.
5. Minimize unnecessary change.

## Build the semantic lock

Identify before editing:

- people, organizations, products, places, laws, identifiers, and protected terminology;
- numbers, percentages, prices, units, dates, times, ranges, versions, and comparisons;
- negation and restrictions;
- possibility, probability, recommendation, obligation, permission, and certainty;
- attribution, quotation content, citations, URLs, code, commands, and filenames;
- causal and temporal relationships;
- promises, deadlines, responsibility, and the writer's stated opinion or experience.

Separate substantive, attributable claims from metadiscourse and unsupported evaluation. When the user asks to preserve facts, do not automatically treat *kluczowy*, *intuicyjny*, *kompleksowy*, or similar promotional judgments as locked facts. Preserve them when the user identifies them as approved claims, quotations, house voice, or required wording; otherwise remove or qualify them if they carry no supported information.

Reject a fluent candidate if any locked element changes. Semantic similarity is not enough: *Aktualizacja może zmniejszyć zużycie pamięci o 15%* must not become *Aktualizacja zmniejsza zużycie pamięci o 15%*.

Preserve the content of a quotation and its speaker. Normalize surrounding typography only when the task permits it. Preserve source spelling inside a quotation unless the user asks to correct or silently normalize quotations.

## Translate, then edit

Translate every relevant proposition before improving style. Preserve qualifications, paragraph relationships, terminology, and unresolved ambiguity. Keep names, product labels, code, URLs, citations, and quoted source text in their required form.

Avoid source-language syntax when Polish expresses the relationship differently. Prefer an established Polish collocation over a literal calque, but do not replace a domain term merely because a common-language alternative sounds simpler.

## Make the smallest sufficient edit

For each problem, prefer the candidate that:

1. preserves every locked element;
2. corrects the actual defect;
3. fits the genre and voice;
4. introduces the least unsupported interpretation;
5. changes no more than necessary.

Do not manufacture concrete details to replace vague source material. If *innowacyjne rozwiązanie* has no supported mechanism or benefit, remove or qualify the empty evaluation; do not invent product capabilities.

Preserve repetition when it maintains legal precision, terminology, emphasis, rhythm, or reference clarity. Preserve passive and impersonal forms when the process, method, or result legitimately matters more than the actor.
