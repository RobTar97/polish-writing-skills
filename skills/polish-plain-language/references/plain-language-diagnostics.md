# Plain-language diagnostics

Use these as contextual review prompts, not mechanical replacement rules.

## Information architecture

Inspect whether:

- the institution's process appears before the reader's result or action;
- a legal basis delays the information the reader needs;
- the required action is buried late in a paragraph;
- prerequisites appear after the step they control;
- conditions, exceptions, and consequences are separated or implicit;
- headings such as *Informacje ogólne* or *Pozostałe informacje* conceal the reader question;
- a paragraph performs several unrelated jobs;
- important help or recovery information is difficult to locate.

Prefer headings such as *Kto może złożyć wniosek*, *Co musisz przygotować*, *Jak złożyć wniosek*, *Ile zapłacisz*, *Co zrobić po odmowie*.

## Bureaucratic constructions

Inspect nominalizations and institutional frames such as:

| Pattern | Prefer when meaning permits |
| --- | --- |
| dokonać wpłaty | wpłacić |
| dokonać zgłoszenia | zgłosić |
| dokonać wyboru | wybrać |
| przeprowadzić weryfikację | sprawdzić / zweryfikować |
| w celu uzyskania | aby uzyskać |
| istnieje możliwość | możesz / można |
| zachodzi konieczność | musisz / trzeba |
| nastąpi realizacja | zrealizujemy / rozpocznie się |

Do not perform blind substitution. Name the actor when the reader needs to know who is responsible; retain an impersonal form when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded.

Question openings such as *Uprzejmie informujemy, że*, *Niniejszym zawiadamiamy*, *W związku z*, *Mając na uwadze* and *Na podstawie*. Retain them when they carry relationship, evidentiary, or legal value; otherwise lead with the result or action.

## Conditions and reference

Model procedural logic as:

```text
IF condition
THEN action
BY deadline
OTHERWISE consequence
```

Realize it naturally in Polish. A condition may correctly appear first. Never impose a rigid subject–verb–object order.

Replace vague references such as *ww.*, *powyższy*, *przedmiotowy*, *wskazany wyżej*, or *niniejszy* with the exact noun when that improves retrieval. Repeat the stable term when a pronoun or generic *dokument* would be ambiguous.

## Terminology and abbreviations

- Keep one label for one concept.
- Do not rotate among *wniosek*, *formularz*, *podanie*, *zgłoszenie*, and *dokument* unless the source distinguishes them.
- Retain a necessary term and add a concise explanation: `odwołanie, czyli formalne zakwestionowanie decyzji`.
- Expand an unfamiliar abbreviation on first meaningful use when helpful.
- Preserve literal UI labels exactly and distinguish them typographically when format permits.

## Sentences

Prefer one dominant proposition per sentence, clear attachment, and modest embedding. Inspect:

- unusually long sentences;
- several subordinate clauses or parentheticals;
- long noun or genitive chains;
- distant actors and verbs;
- multiple actions in one instruction;
- ambiguous pronouns;
- choppy fragments that obscure causality or conditions.

An average of roughly 15–20 words may be a useful tendency for public information, but no sentence-length threshold defines plain Polish. Split only when the semantic relationship remains visible.

## Headings, lists, and warnings

Use headings to predict useful content. Use lists for sequences, requirements, options, comparisons, or genuinely parallel items—not as decoration.

For warnings, expose:

1. the risk;
2. the condition that triggers it;
3. the consequence;
4. the safe or corrective action, when known.

For errors and service messages, answer what happened, what it means, what the reader can do, and what happens next. Never invent reassurance such as `Twoja karta nie została obciążona` unless the source or system state establishes it.

## Plain language and accessibility

Plain wording can improve understandable labels, instructions, headings, warnings, errors, and recovery guidance. It cannot by itself establish semantic markup, keyboard access, contrast, assistive-technology compatibility, or WCAG conformance.
